## Amendment One — the steward's broadcast-and-preservation duty

**What happened, as record.** In July 2026 the stewarding nonprofit executed its Open Source Software Broadcast and Preservation Policy (P2-003) across the fourteen public repositories of its R&D organization: a continental mirror network (Codeberg for Europe, Gitee for Asia under Presidential directive, SourceHut and GitLab for North America, Bitbucket for Oceania, with South American and African community forges deferred until localization exists); daily automated synchronization with verified commit parity across every platform; Developer Certificate of Origin enforcement on the primary repository — sign-off required at the web surface, a DCO check blocking every merge; quarterly scheduled releases; and the preservation tier — Software Heritage archival, Internet Archive items carrying both a source snapshot and the full git history as a restorable bundle, Zenodo DOIs staged to mint at each release. The operational records live in `Operations/Broadcast Preservation/` ([process](../Operations/Broadcast%20Preservation/oss-broadcast-and-preservation-process.md), [distribution matrix](../Operations/Broadcast%20Preservation/distribution-matrix.md), [preservation record](../Operations/Broadcast%20Preservation/preservation-broadcast-record.md)).

**Why this is governance-layer work, not publicity.** The architecture already states the principle this practice serves: *a license guarantees the right to fork; it cannot supply the capacity* (Part I). Legibility makes the expensive exit possible; broadcast and preservation make it **cheap enough to be real**. Each independent, continuously-synchronized copy of the canon is one more place the community can leave *to* — and one more independent observer of what the canonical history is. This is MOD applied to the artifact layer: with mirrors on five platforms in five jurisdictions plus three archival systems, quietly rewriting the record of the code itself becomes as expensive as the witnessed record makes rewriting the ledger. A single-host canon is a hosting chokepoint wearing a license; the mirror network is the exit-capacity the license only promises.

**Preservation takes unfinished work.** The doctrine settled in execution: archival tiers (Software Heritage, Internet Archive) receive every repository from inception — seeds included — because archiving adds durability and provenance to already-public work without adding exposure, and a timestamped third-party archive establishes authorship for early-stage specifications. Promotion tiers (DOIs, package registries, discovery platforms) wait for earned releases. *Anchor a claim, never certify it*, applied to the canon's own artifacts: the archive attests occurrence, never quality.

**Provenance discipline, made mechanical.** The governance layer already names provenance — copyleft commons, developer certificate of origin — as a mechanism (Part II, Governance row). The July 2026 work upgraded it from stated rule to enforced floor: sign-off is now a build-blocking check, not an honor system. Per the legibility ladder's own logic (rung 5 — prose bound to code by checks that can fail), a provenance rule that cannot fail a merge is marketing; now it can fail the merge.

**Recommended pattern, not mandate.** Mirror-network topology, platform choice, and archival cadence are steward practice — policy, not spec. Mandating a platform list in the core would grow the contested prize and date the document. What binds is the duty; how it is discharged stays diffable policy (rung 2).

**Conformance delta (proposed).** The legibility check (standard #7) gains a clause: *…and its canon is preserved and broadcast redundantly enough that forking it does not depend on any single host's continued goodwill — fork-capacity is provisioned, not merely permitted.*

## Amendment Two — multilingual legibility

**What happened, as record.** The steward's Global Multilingual Broadcast Policy (P2-002, resting on the P3-005 language analysis) commits the canon and its educational surfaces to localization across a portfolio now standing at seven languages: Spanish, Hindi, Chinese (Mandarin), Portuguese, Arabic — and, by Presidential directive of 2026-07-24 pending Board ratification, **French** (the UN-institution and Francophonie bridge) and **Toki Pona** (a niche linguistic community). Stored, as amended, in [Operations/Multilingual Broadcast/](../Operations/Multilingual%20Broadcast/p2-002-global-multilingual-broadcast-policy.md).

**Why this is a legibility mechanism.** Standard #7 says the governed can *read* the logic that governs them. A commons documented in one language is legible to one linguistic community and opaque to every other it claims to serve — the same asymmetry the architecture diagnoses at the interface, reproduced at the language layer. Multilingual broadcast is the legibility invariant crossing linguistic boundaries; and linguistically diverse readers are *diverse observers* in the MOD sense — a mistranslation, like an equivocation, grows expensive to maintain as independent bilingual readers multiply. The policy's own discipline (community translation under trusted regional review, terminology glossaries, translation memory) is rung-2 thinking: language versions as diffable policy, not forked prose.

**The Toki Pona note.** A ~120-word language cannot carry jargon; rendering a document into it is a forcing function for plain-language source text. The cheapest member of the portfolio is also a working audit of rung 4 — *the spec is the constitution, written in prose* a motivated lay reader can follow. If a normative sentence cannot survive translation into a minimal language, that is evidence about the sentence.

**Bilingual documentation is conformant.** Core specifications may remain in English while tutorials and community discussion localize (P2-002 §2.2) — consistent with the ladder, where rung 5 binds the normative text to code and translations bind to the normative text, never the reverse. One source of truth; many renders. Presentation sovereignty (Amendment 001) at the language layer.

**Conformance delta (proposed).** None to the standard. A sentence added to the legibility ladder's introduction: *interpretive accessibility includes linguistic accessibility; a translation is a render bound to the normative text, never a second source of truth.*

## Amendment Three — entity types and the stack (an exploration)

*Held to every invariant above; mandates nothing. The question: the current steward is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit — could an NGO, a public benefit corporation, a C corporation, an LLC, or a cooperative do this work?*

**The architecture's own answer-shape.** The document already contains the sorting principle: *govern every layer by the cost of leaving it* — and *architecture over ownership* (Part I): ownership is downstream of architecture, and cooperative ownership of an extractive architecture reproduces extraction. So the entity-type question decomposes by layer, and legal form is never sufficient. The honest question is not "which entity types are virtuous" but "which entity types can credibly hold which layer."

**At the exit-governed layers — any entity type.** Front ends, operators, witnesses, and hosts live where exit is cheap by construction: the market, matching, and record sit in the protocol, so leaving one provider costs only that provider. Exit disciplines whatever it can reach — including a profit motive. A C corporation running a front end, an LLC operating a node, a PBC hosting a witness: all conformant, because the architecture's floors (witnessed record, non-convertible credit, no-averaging covenant, presentation sovereignty) bind them structurally, and a member who dislikes their trajectory leaves on the trend. This is the design working as intended: the stack does not need its participants to be saints; it needs their departure to be cheap. The conformance suite, not the charter, is the gate.

**At the core — the constraint bites.** The core steward holds the one layer no one can leave, disciplined only by entrenchment, the expensive fork, and standing internal opposition (Part III, Governance). The stewardship requirements are structural: a named, internally-accountable body; a board filled from the bottom with recallable delegates of open circles; deepest commitments double-locked; *capturing the core must pay little*. That last clause is where entity type stops being neutral:

- **Nonprofit / NGO forms** carry a **non-distribution constraint**: no equity, no residual claimant, no one to whom monetized capture can be paid out. The form does not guarantee good stewardship — capture-by-salary and board entrenchment are real nonprofit failure modes, which is exactly why the recallable-delegate and double-lock machinery exists — but the form removes the *standing gradient* toward monetizing the no-exit layer. The 501(c)(3) variant adds an external regulator (mission lock, private-inurement prohibition) as a cheap second witness on the steward itself. NGO equivalents vary by jurisdiction; what matters is that the governance instrument can bind board composition to recallable open-circle delegates — most nonprofit statutes permit this; verify per jurisdiction.
- **PBC** softens but does not remove the gradient: a benefit purpose sits alongside shareholders who retain residual claims and, in every PBC statute, enforcement of the benefit purpose is weak while enforcement of shareholder rights is strong. A PBC stewarding the core holds a standing conflict the architecture would have to fight continuously; a PBC at an exit-governed layer is unremarkable.
- **C corporation / LLC** have fiduciary or contractual duties running to owners — a standing incentive to make the core pay, which is precisely what the minimal-core discipline exists to prevent. An LLC's operating agreement is flexible enough to *simulate* the nonprofit constraints (no distributions, delegate-controlled membership), but a simulation that owners can amend by the same flexibility that created it is entrenchment without the lock. Conformant as operators; structurally miscast as core steward.
- **Cooperative** is the instructive case the document already litigates: member ownership of an extractive architecture reproduces extraction. A co-op stewarding a conformant core adds little that the open-circle/recallable-delegate machinery doesn't already provide, and its equity-like member accounts reintroduce a mild residual-claimant problem. Conformant, with the same vigilance as any steward.

**Stated as a rule of thumb.** The architecture is **entity-type-agnostic wherever exit governs, and entity-type-opinionated only at the core** — and even there, the opinion is not "must be a nonprofit" but "must carry a non-distribution constraint and bottom-up recallable composition, natively or by entrenchment at least as strong as the double lock." Nonprofit forms supply this natively; for-profit forms can only simulate it against their own fiduciary gradient. An instance choosing a for-profit core steward is making the same class of move as a proprietary governance venue: conformant, if at all, only as a named interim with a committed exit.

**What the steward's own July 2026 activity illustrates.** The broadcast-and-preservation work (Amendment One) is what a non-distribution steward *spends on*: provisioning the community's exit capacity — an expenditure with no return except the architecture holding. That spending pattern is the practical signature distinguishing a steward from an owner, whatever the tax form says.

## Governance observations and open questions

- **Directive-then-ratify.** Two July 2026 portfolio decisions (Gitee's reinstatement against published P2-003 text; the French and Toki Pona additions to P2-002) were made by Presidential directive with Board ratification pending. The architecture's own discipline applies: these are conformant as **named interims on a committed path** — visible, recorded in the operational documents, and standing violations if ratification never comes. The pattern itself is worth a policy sentence somewhere in the steward's instrument: which offices may act ahead of the Board, on what classes of decision, with what reporting clock (P2-003 §12.3's emergency-update machinery is the nearest existing analogue).
- **The mirror network is operated from one machine.** Synchronization for all fourteen repositories currently runs from a single steward-controlled host on scheduled tasks. As substrate practice this is a mild concentration — the mirrors themselves are the redundancy, but the *pump* is singular. Watch for: a second, independently-operated sync host, or platform-native pull mirrors where free tiers allow.
- **Should the mirror network be witnessed?** Release hashes are already slated for the witnessed record (legibility ladder, rung 7). Whether mirror-parity attestations belong there too — a countersigned "these six heads matched at time T" — is open, and cheap to add once lifecycle witnessing (open problem 2) is real.
- **Entity-type verification per jurisdiction.** Amendment Three's NGO paragraph generalizes from US law; the recallable-delegate binding needs per-jurisdiction legal reads before any non-US instance leans on it.
- **Localization conformance.** When translated normative text ships, rung 5 needs a translation-conformance answer — what check fails the build when a translation drifts? Undecided; belongs to P2-002 §2.3's quality machinery.
